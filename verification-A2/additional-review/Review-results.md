# Additional verification and fixes — A2 / A2-Economy

The additional audit found a real gap in reset handling and corrected it in both board variants. The previous firmware comment incorrectly said that hardware held the USB hub in reset during MCU reset: R13 actually pulled the hub reset high. A2 adds hardware supply supervision and an independent reset gate. This is a stronger prototype release, not a guarantee that an assembled board will work or a declaration that every possible fault has been analyzed.

Both variants contain the same 161 components and 44 part types. The economy board retains its lower-cost fabrication choices. No order or payment has been made. The A1 quote and online cart have not been verified against A2; use the new BOM and purchase files when obtaining the next quote.

**What changed**

- U34 (TPS3808G33DBVR) monitors the 3.3 V supply. U35 (TPS3808G50DBVR) monitors 5 V while drawing its own power from 3.3 V. Both can hold MCU_NRST low. The second arrangement matters if HDMI or a host partially powers the core while the board's normal 5 V supply is missing.
- U36 (SN74LVC1G97DBVR), configured as an AND gate with Schmitt inputs, releases the hub reset only when MCU_NRST and the MCU's separate HUB_RESET_REQ are high. R49 holds that request low while the MCU pin is undriven. Programming, startup and watchdog reset no longer rely on firmware having executed before the hub is held in reset. PA9 remains push-pull and is connected to a gate input, not tied to another output.
- C53–C55 decouple the three new ICs. R50 adds 1 kohm in the ground leg of the existing 100 nF reset capacitor C8. This retains a series-RC reset filter and limits the bulk capacitor's discharge through the supervisors. Its filter response differs from the previous direct capacitor, so reset/noise testing remains necessary.
- Clear portions of the 3.3 V routing were widened. The geometry-based positive-rail model now estimates approximately 31 mV maximum loss at approximately 207 mA, compared with approximately 39 mV previously. These calculations assume copper thickness, temperature and current distribution; they exclude contact resistance, ground offset and dynamic effects.
- The firmware's initialization is now directly testable, and its comments identify the actual reset circuit. Unused GPIO pins are now configured as analog inputs to avoid floating digital-input current, while active control pins and SWD are preserved. This follows ST's I/O-current guidance; the startup tests check these modes. The switching algorithm is unchanged. A fresh embedded build is 980 bytes, with 4 bytes of static RAM, and is included with its source and tests.

The reset circuit was checked against the [TI supervisor datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps3808.pdf), the [TI gate datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc1g97.pdf) and the [STM32 datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32f030c8.pdf). The gate choice specifically accommodates the slow edges on the capacitor-loaded reset line. These additions are recovery and startup safeguards; they are not isolation devices or a guarantee of uninterrupted operation during a supply failure.

The artwork checker was also given two deliberately defective inputs: a copper bridge across R1 and an omitted USB_UP_P plated-hole connection. It detected the resulting short and open. This avoids relying on a checker that only happens to produce a passing result. Raster extraction has finite resolution and is additional evidence, not a replacement for DRC or factory electrical testing.

A diagnostic run enabled the normally ignored track-centering rule and cleared any DRC exclusions. The 12 resulting track-centering warnings concern ground or power connections, not high-speed signals. They represent off-center attachment to copper that is electrically connected; the artwork extraction confirms connectivity. The normal project rule remains unchanged, and the diagnostic report is retained. The test runtime could not run AddressSanitizer on this host; undefined-behavior sanitizer and the other stated tests did run successfully.

The opposite-side mechanical screen initially flagged two HDMI shield leads using a deliberately oversized circular envelope. Using their actual rotated slot shapes, plus a 0.25 mm allowance, gives nominal clearance to the opposite courtyards of approximately 0.055 mm at R30 and 0.175 mm at U20. These are small nominal margins to a conservative envelope, not a tolerance analysis of solder fillets or connector leads. Inspect these locations during assembly; trim protruding leads only if appropriate for the connector and workmanship. No exact enclosure or complete supplier 3D model has been supplied.

**Power-state and fault findings**

[TI documents that HD3SS215 can back-power its supply through TMDS protection structures when its supply is off](https://e2e.ti.com/support/interface-group/interface/f/interface-forum/401353/hd3ss215-leak-current-when-vcc-is-off). TI also states that the described powered-off TMDS condition is not a problem for the chip. The audit therefore treats this as a partial-power/startup condition to manage, not proof of impending damage. The new 5 V qualification prevents treating such a partial supply as normal operation. It does not remove the injection path.

The [TS3USB221 datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ts3usb221.pdf) explicitly specifies powered-off leakage, unlike the HD3SS215 standby-current specification. Host VBUS dividers have no direct connection between the two hosts' power sources. Their upper-resistor limit bounds available injection to approximately 56 microamps per host if the core is unpowered; the actual core voltage then depends on other circuitry. Port power-switch reverse-blocking conditions were reviewed as conditions, not assumed to be ideal diodes under every fault.

At 3.6 V, including R50 tolerance and a 65 °C resistor-temperature allowance, the reset capacitor branch contributes at most approximately 3.66 mA. Adding the bounded DC pullups and input leakage gives approximately 4.15 mA, below the supervisor's 5 mA absolute output-current limit. This calculation bounds that bulk-capacitor path; it does not claim to model fast parasitic-capacitance spikes. The resistor's calculated peak dissipation is approximately 13.2 mW versus its 62.5 mW rating.

The supervisor's nominal open-CT release delay is bounded at 12–28 ms in its stated conditions. Its 3.3 V falling threshold spans approximately 3.024–3.116 V over the full specified temperature range; a conservative maximum rising threshold is approximately 3.194 V. Because the sense point is near the regulator and remote IC supplies lose voltage in copper and ground, this does **not** guarantee that every remote 3.0 V device remains within its operating range until reset asserts. The 5 V monitor similarly provides recovery qualification, not a USB-output-voltage compliance guarantee during a brownout. Rapid ramps, interrupted power and capacitor-loaded reset edges require measurement.

Four USB power switches can jointly demand more than the buck's 3 A continuous rating during faults or capacitor charging; their current limits are not fixed 500 mA clamps. The [TPS2051C family datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps2051c.pdf) gives 0.67–1.01 A for the 0.5 A variant. The normal four-port continuous budget remains 2 A plus board/HDMI loads. Simultaneous startup, short-circuit recovery, supply collapse and possible restart cycling are still physical acceptance tests. No claim of a validated load-step waveform is made.

**What remains unverified**

The earlier detailed field, DC-bias, crosstalk and thermal studies remain available in `outputs/Economy-additional-review`. Their limits still apply. In particular, removing exposed-pad thermal vias creates thermal uncertainty. The available thermal screens do not establish a safe full-load ambient-temperature rating, and the AP2112 temperature coefficient is typical rather than a guaranteed maximum. No exact full-channel HDMI/USB model with validated connector, cable and device data is available; the previously downloaded mismatched TI S-parameter model was excluded from acceptance.

An assembled prototype is still needed for thermal measurements; power-on/brownout/reset waveforms; all-port inrush, load steps and fault recovery; USB enumeration/hot-plug; both USB-C orientations; HDMI EDID/DDC/HPD and the actual two-monitor/cable combinations; ESD/EMI behavior; and solder/connector/mechanical inspection. The intended target remains 1440p60 plus 1080p60. This is not a promise of every resolution, cable, display or computer combination.

No final JLC factory DFM or impedance report has been obtained. The free DFM service required a separate signed-in session, and no result has been returned. A supplier's approval, exact stackup acceptance and refreshed delivered price remain separate from these checks. No supplier message, order or payment was sent. There may be further insight available from an independent hardware engineer or more complete vendor/channel models; this report does not call the design the maximum verification possible in principle.

Use the A2-Economy project, Gerbers and BOM together. The A2 original is the same circuit with the more expensive thermal-via/fabrication choices. Earlier A0/A1 releases are historical.
